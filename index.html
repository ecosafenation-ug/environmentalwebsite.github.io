Uganda Weather & Climate

Uganda is a very beautiful country found in the Eastern part of africa and experiences a very unique climatic type.

📆 3-Day Weather Outlook

🌦️ Uganda's Unique Climate

Uganda enjoys a tropical climate with two rainy seasons (March-May & September-November). Average temperatures range from 18°C to 28°C. Due to climate change, unpredictable heavy rainfall and dry spells are becoming more common. Eco Safe Nation promotes climate adaptation through reforestation and sustainable water management.

Local Resources

Uganda is the "Pearl of Africa," rich in natural capital that drives our economy and survival.

Water Resources

Lake Victoria, the source of the River Nile, and vast wetlands regulate our weather and provide water for life.

Forests & Wildlife

From Mabira to Bwindi, our forests are home to gorillas and chimpanzees, acting as vital lungs for the planet.

Fertile Soils

Our volcanic soils support agriculture, feeding the nation through crops like Matooke, Coffee, and Maize.

Minerals

Copper, cobalt, gold, and the recently discovered oil reserves in the Albertine Graben.

Biodiversity

Uganda is a global biodiversity hotspot, hosting over 18,783 recorded species of flora and fauna, including 53.9% of the world's mountain gorillas and over 1,000 bird species (50% of Africa’s total)

Efficient Utilization

Resources are finite. We must use them wisely today so they exist for our children tomorrow.Resource utilization in Uganda focuses on harnessing abundant natural, agricultural, and human resources to drive economic growth and improve livelihoods, though it faces significant challenges regarding sustainability and efficiency. While the country is rich in water bodies, fertile soil, and minerals, many resources are underutilized or overexploited due to inefficient practices, leading to calls for better governance and sustainable management.

Renewable Energy

Shifting from charcoal to solar power and biogas protects our forests and reduces indoor air pollution.

Sustainable Farming

Using organic manure and crop rotation maintains soil health without damaging the land with harsh chemicals.

Water Harvesting

Collecting rainwater reduces the strain on Lake Victoria and ensures water availability during dry seasons.

Community Development

Environmental protection starts at the grassroots level with the people of Uganda. Community development in Uganda focuses on empowering local communities to identify needs, utilize resources, and create sustainable, citizen-driven solutions for improved quality of life. Key initiatives include strengthening local governance, promoting economic empowerment (e.g., SACCOs), improving agriculture, and fostering social protection, largely coordinated by Community Development Officers (CDOs).

Air Pollution

The invisible danger affecting the health of Ugandans in cities and villages. In accordance with the WHO's guidelines, the air quality in Uganda is considered unsafe with the most recent data indicating the country's annual mean concentration of PM2. 5 is 50 µg/m3 which exceeds the recommended maximum of 10 µg/m3

⚠️ Causes

  • Old vehicle emissions (boda-bodas) in Kampala.
  • Burning of charcoal and firewood.
  • Open burning of plastic waste.
  • Dust from construction and road works.

💥 Impacts

  • High rates of asthma and respiratory infections.
  • Contributes to climate change.
  • Reduced life expectancy.
  • Smog reduces visibility.

✅ Solutions

  • Promote electric vehicles and public transport.
  • Use energy-efficient cookstoves.
  • Enforce laws against burning trash.
  • Plant trees along city streets.

Water Pollution

Threatening the purity of Lake Victoria, the Nile, and our wetlands. Water pollution in Uganda is a severe crisis, with 47% of key water sources (boreholes, springs) contaminated and unsafe, causing over 70% of reported diseases like cholera and typhoid. Major threats include industrial waste, agricultural runoff, untreated sewage, and rampant plastic pollution, primarily affecting Lake Victoria and urban water bodies.

⚠️ Causes

  • Industrial waste dumped into rivers.
  • Runoff of fertilizers from farms.
  • Improper disposal of plastics (Kaveera).
  • Silting from deforested riverbanks.

💥 Impacts

  • Waterborne diseases (Cholera, Typhoid).
  • Death of fish and aquatic life.
  • Expensive water treatment costs.
  • Loss of tourism revenue.

✅ Solutions

  • Protect wetlands as natural filters.
  • Ban single-use plastics.
  • Treat industrial waste before release.
  • Community riverbank restoration.

Land & Soil Pollution

Degrading the fertile ground we rely on for food and shelter. Land and soil pollution in Uganda is a severe crisis, with approximately 46% of land degraded due to deforestation, poor farming practices, overgrazing, and uncontrolled industrial/plastic waste dumping. This degradation, driven by high population growth and urbanization, reduces soil fertility, decreases agricultural productivity, and threatens food security, costing the economy roughly 17% of GDP.

⚠️ Causes

  • Overflowing landfills and garbage dumps.
  • Excessive use of chemical pesticides.
  • Improper disposal of electronic waste.
  • Oil spills in the Albertine region.

💥 Impacts

  • Loss of soil fertility (lower crop yields).
  • Soil erosion and landslides (e.g., Bududa).
  • Toxic chemicals entering the food chain.
  • Unsightly heaps of rubbish.

✅ Solutions

  • Practice composting organic waste.
  • Switch to organic farming.
  • Recycle and reuse materials.
  • Reforestation to hold soil together.

FAQs

Got questions about Uganda’s environment, resource efficiency, pollution, or how you can make a difference? We’ve got answers.

1. What are the main environmental challenges facing Uganda today?

Uganda faces several urgent environmental threats including:

  • Deforestation: Over 200,000 hectares of forest lost annually due to charcoal burning and agricultural expansion.
  • Air pollution: PM2.5 levels (50 µg/m³) far exceed WHO safe limits, mainly from old vehicles and biomass burning.
  • Water contamination: 47% of water sources are polluted by industrial waste, sewage, and plastic (kaveera).
  • Land degradation: Nearly 46% of land is degraded, causing low crop yields and landslides (e.g., Bududa).

Eco Safe Nation works on all fronts to raise awareness and drive community-led solutions.

2. How does water pollution affect communities in Uganda?

Polluted water directly impacts health, economy, and daily life. Common consequences include:

  • Outbreaks of waterborne diseases such as cholera, typhoid, and dysentery – leading causes of child mortality.
  • High costs for water treatment and scarcity of safe drinking water in rural areas.
  • Death of fish and aquatic species, threatening the livelihoods of fishing communities along Lake Victoria and the Nile.
  • Loss of tourism revenue due to polluted beaches and floating plastic waste.

Our clean-up campaigns and advocacy for plastic bans aim to restore Uganda’s water bodies.

3. What is "efficient resource utilization" and why does it matter?

Efficient resource utilization means using natural resources (water, forests, soil, energy) wisely without waste or overexploitation. In Uganda, this includes:

  • Renewable energy: Switching from charcoal to solar and biogas to protect forests and reduce indoor air pollution.
  • Sustainable farming: Using crop rotation, mulching, and organic manure instead of harmful chemicals to preserve soil fertility.
  • Rainwater harvesting: Collecting rain reduces stress on Lake Victoria and ensures water during dry seasons.

Efficient use ensures future generations can also thrive from Uganda’s natural wealth. Learn more in our Utilization section →

4. How can ordinary citizens help reduce air pollution in Kampala and other cities?

Every Ugandan can take simple but powerful actions:

  • Walk, bike, or use public transport – fewer boda-bodas and old cars on the road reduce harmful emissions.
  • Switch to energy-saving cookstoves and avoid burning waste (especially plastics).
  • Plant trees along streets and homesteads – trees absorb pollutants and provide shade.
  • Report open burning of garbage to local authorities and support campaigns for electric mobility.

Eco Safe Nation organizes awareness workshops and tree-planting events — join us to make a difference.

5. What community initiatives does Eco Safe Nation support?

We believe local action drives lasting change. Our key community programs include:

  • Youth Conservation Clubs: Environmental education in primary & secondary schools across Gulu, Kampala, and Jinja.
  • Women’s Green Groups: Training in briquette-making, organic farming, and upcycling waste to reduce deforestation and poverty.
  • Clean-up campaigns: Monthly clean-ups of drainage channels, markets, and Lake Victoria shores.
  • Eco-tourism advocacy: Helping local communities benefit from gorilla and bird conservation while protecting biodiversity.

6. Are there any government policies addressing pollution in Uganda?

Yes, Uganda has several environmental laws, though enforcement remains a challenge. Key policies include:

  • The National Environment Act, 2019 – provides for NEMA (National Environment Management Authority) to regulate pollution and waste.
  • Ban on single-use plastics (kaveera) – though still poorly enforced, efforts to revive the ban are ongoing.
  • Uganda Green Growth Development Strategy – aims to promote renewable energy, sustainable agriculture, and clean production.
  • Wetlands protection regulations – restrict encroachment and dumping in critical ecosystems.

Eco Safe Nation works with local councils and NEMA to strengthen community oversight and reporting mechanisms.

8. How does land pollution affect agriculture and food security?

Land and soil pollution is a silent crisis for Uganda’s mainly agricultural economy. Consequences include:

  • Loss of soil fertility from chemical overuse and erosion – leading to reduced yields of matooke, maize, and coffee.
  • Contamination of crops with heavy metals (from e-waste dumps and industrial runoff), posing health risks.
  • Landslides in highland areas (e.g., Bududa, Elgon region) due to deforestation and poor land management.
  • Economic loss: land degradation costs Uganda roughly 17% of GDP annually (UN report).

Our solutions promote composting, agroforestry, and responsible waste disposal to restore soil health.

9. What is Uganda’s biodiversity importance and how is it threatened?

Uganda is a global biodiversity hotspot, home to 53.9% of the world’s remaining mountain gorillas, over 1,000 bird species, and countless unique plants. However, threats are severe:

  • Habitat destruction due to agricultural expansion, illegal logging, and settlement encroachment.
  • Poaching & wildlife trafficking – affecting gorillas, elephants, and chimpanzees.
  • Climate change – altering rainfall patterns and affecting migratory bird routes.

Eco Safe Nation promotes eco-tourism that directly funds conservation and gives communities economic incentives to protect wildlife.

10. How can schools and students get involved?

  • Inviting our team for environmental talks and documentary screenings.
  • Competing in the annual “Green Schools” competition (posters, essays, and cleanest compound awards).
  • Conducting mini-research projects on local pollution sources (air/water quality tests).
